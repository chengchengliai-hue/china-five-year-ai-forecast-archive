# Fixed questionnaire and shared instruction

Use this questionnaire unchanged across annual runs. Replace only dates, the model record, and the dated source pack.

## Ordinary-resident definition

Use this fixed definition:

> A resident of China who relies mainly on their own or family members’ labor income and does not possess large capital holdings, special power, inside information, or stable passive income.

This is an economic position, not a fictional single person. When distribution matters, discuss at least: a new labor-market entrant, a mid-career employee, a skilled technical worker, a small business operator, and a household carrying child or elder-care duties.

## Fixed twelve questions

- `P01` By the target date, has ordinary residents’ real income and purchasing power improved?
- `P02` Is it easier or harder to obtain and retain suitable, stable, reasonably paid work with room to grow?
- `P03` Which broad capabilities have gained or lost labor-market value?
- `P04` Which broad occupational families have expanded or contracted?
- `P05` Which kinds of industries have spread benefits to ordinary workers rather than only increasing output, profit, valuation, or exports?
- `P06` What role does housing play in household security, wealth, mobility, marriage, education, and city choice?
- `P07` How have childrearing, education, healthcare, elder care, and household time burdens changed?
- `P08` How have opportunities shifted across city clusters, provincial capitals, industrial nodes, smaller cities, counties, and rural areas?
- `P09` Has technology mostly augmented ordinary workers, displaced tasks and entry jobs, or weakened bargaining power?
- `P10` Which routes to upward mobility remain realistic, and which traditional routes have weakened?
- `P11` Does the external environment expand or compress ordinary residents’ opportunities, and through which trade, technology, finance, energy, or supply-chain channels?
- `P12` What three structural changes affecting ordinary residents are currently underestimated?

## Shared instruction

Give every specialist this instruction verbatim, with bracketed values filled:

> The information cutoff is [CUTOFF] in [TIMEZONE]. The forecast target is [TARGET DATE]. Use no information published after the cutoff. Forecast the structural state at the target date, not yearly fluctuations between the cutoff and target. Analyze from the perspective of the fixed ordinary-resident definition. Answer P01–P12. Treat economic, industrial, policy, demographic, technological, regional, and external changes as drivers; treat work, income, purchasing power, family burden, housing security, and mobility as outcomes. Do not turn policy targets into achieved results. For every major prediction, provide supporting facts, a causal mechanism, affected groups, the strongest counterargument, failure conditions, and an observable validation method. Use probability only once in the compact registry; the report body must focus on substantive analysis.

## Stable direction vocabulary

Use one of:

- strong improvement;
- modest improvement;
- broadly unchanged;
- modest deterioration;
- strong deterioration;
- structural shift with mixed welfare effects.

Do not force a positive/negative label when the claim is genuinely distributive or structural.
